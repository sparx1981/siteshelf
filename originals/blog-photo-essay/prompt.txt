Build a single-page "Photo Essay" blog template for an invented photography magazine, "Lantern Hours": a visual story about a night market, told in eleven plates across four chapters, with varied plate layouts, captions, a chapter title that changes as you scroll, and a full-screen viewer. Everything is one HTML file and the "photographs" are painted illustrations.

Canvas & base: A dark gallery wall. Page #121014 with a barely visible warm vignette, text #efe9df (14.8:1), secondary #b9b0a3 (8.3:1), lantern orange #ffa23f (9.1:1 on the page; dark text #1a1208 on it 9.4:1), hairline #2c2830. Plates sit on the wall with thin #3a343e frames and a soft drop shadow. Invented magazine, market and people only.

Typography: "Gloock" 400 for the title (clamp 44 to 108px, tight 0.95 leading), the chapter numerals (clamp 64 to 140px) and chapter names; "Karla" 400/500/700 for captions, labels and UI at 14 to 17px; captions use 15px/1.55 with the plate number in bold lantern orange small caps. Eyebrow 12px, 0.2em tracking, uppercase, orange.

Imagery & art direction: Every plate is painted from the scene library, graded like film (grain, a vignette and warm highlights): (1) a wide night city and market skyline with strings of lit windows; (2) a river at dusk with lantern reflections (sea generator, dusk palette); (3) a portrait of a noodle vendor (portrait generator, 3:4) beside a caption; (4) a large overhead bowl of noodles with chillies and herbs (food generator); (5) a diptych of two plates of skewers and dumplings; (6) an abstract steam-and-smoke painting in oranges and charcoal (abstract generator, 21:9); (7) a pair of vendor portraits side by side; (8) the interior of a covered stall with hanging bulbs (interior generator); (9) a small still life of a sauce bottle (product generator) with a long caption; (10) the market at first light, empty chairs and a pale sky (city generator, dawn palette, wide); (11) a calm dawn sea with a single lantern, closing the story. Media slots for real photography: every plate has a 3:2, 4:5 or 16:9 slot with alt text, a caption and a photographer credit line.

Layout: A hero (full height) with eyebrow "Lantern Hours · Photo essay", title "The Night Market Between Two Rivers", a byline and a "Scroll for plate 1" cue. Then the plates in four chapters (I Arrival, II Heat, III Faces, IV Closing), each chapter opening with a large numeral and name. Plate layouts vary: full-bleed wide, text-plus-portrait split, two-up diptych, ultra-wide band, centred small still life with a long caption. A fixed chapter marker sits at the left on desktop (numeral, chapter name, "Plate 5 of 11" and a thin vertical progress line) and becomes a slim top bar on mobile. Fixed controls bottom right: Previous plate, Next plate and a "Captions" toggle. Clicking a plate or pressing Enter on it opens the viewer.

Copy: Chapter names: Arrival, Heat, Faces, Closing. Plate captions are short, specific stories (for example "Plate 4. Noodles at 9:40 pm. The vendor adds chillies last, she says, so the bowl arrives with a little argument still in it.") in a warm documentary voice. Messages: "Plate 5 of 11." "Captions hidden." "Captions shown."

Interaction: An IntersectionObserver (or throttled scroll handler) sets the current plate as the one nearest the viewport centre and updates the marker text, cross-fading numeral and name when the chapter changes (200ms; none under reduced motion). Next and Previous scroll to the neighbouring plate (smooth unless reduced motion) and move focus to it; the j and k keys and the Left and Right arrow keys do the same when focus is not in a form field. The Captions toggle (aria-pressed) hides all captions to leave pure images and announces the state. Each plate is a button opening a native dialog viewer showing the plate large with its caption, Previous and Next buttons and a plate counter; Left and Right arrows navigate, Escape closes, focus returns to the plate that opened it. A polite live region announces "Plate 5 of 11" after navigation (not on every scroll tick). Preferences persist in localStorage inside try/catch.

Technique notes: Each plate has a generator function (width, height) that returns the SVG, so the page and the viewer render the same painting at different sizes without id collisions (render the viewer plate fresh each time and clear it on close). The marker progress line height equals scroll progress through the essay. Use scroll-margin-top so plates stop below the top bar on mobile.

Responsive: Under 900px the marker becomes a 44px top bar (numeral, name, counter), the fixed controls shrink to icon buttons with 44px targets, split layouts stack (image first), the diptych stays two-up down to 520px then stacks; no horizontal scroll at 390px.

Rules: Single HTML file, Google Fonts link, inline CSS and JS, no libraries or external images. [hidden]{display:none!important}. Visible focus 3px #efe9df outline with dark offset (on orange buttons use #1a1208). Never rely on colour alone: the marker states the plate and chapter in text. Respect prefers-reduced-motion. Invented names only.
