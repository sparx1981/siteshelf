The case for reading one thing slowly

By Hannah Ellis· 6 min readAnalytics off

There is a particular pleasure in finishing a single long article without once reaching for your phone. It rarely happens by accident. It takes a chair, a cup of something warm and a decision to let the rest of the internet wait.

When we read quickly we collect facts; when we read slowly we collect understanding. The difference shows up days later, when the facts have faded and only the shape of the argument remains. That shape is the thing worth keeping.

Try it this week. Choose one piece, close every other tab, and read it as if you will be asked to explain it to a friend over dinner. You may find you need far fewer articles than you thought.
