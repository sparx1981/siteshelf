Build a single-page "Sunrise Sunset Arc" widget for an invented service, "Halcyon Daylight": a sky scene where a sun travels along its daily arc, with the sunrise, sunset, golden hour and daylight length for a chosen town and date. Everything is computed locally.

Canvas & base: The widget is one large rounded card (max 1000px, 28px radius) on a #0b1026 page. Its top half is a scene: a sky that changes with the sun's height using these keyframes (elevation in degrees: top colour / horizon colour): -18 #05081a / #0d1840, -6 #1d2a66 / #c2477a, 0 #4b3a86 / #ff9a62, 8 #6aa0e0 / #ffd9a0, 30 #3f7fc4 / #cfe6f6, interpolated smoothly. Three layered ridges in the foreground take a darker tint of the horizon colour, with a near-black meadow band at the bottom. Below the scene a panel #0e1430 holds controls and readouts. Text on the panel #eef1ff (15:1), secondary #a7b0d6 (8:1), accent sun-gold #ffd36b, accent dusk #ff9a62, accent night #8fa4ff.

Typography: "Bricolage Grotesque" 600 for the big time readout (clamp 48 to 84px, tabular numerals) and headings; "Inter" 400/500/600 for UI at 14 to 16px; tabular numerals for all times. Eyebrow 12px, 0.18em tracking, uppercase, #ffd36b.

Imagery & art direction: The scene should feel like a wide golden-hour photograph rather than a diagram. Paint it as inline SVG and CSS: the sky gradient with a soft radial glow around the sun that grows warmer near the horizon, 5 to 7 thin cloud ellipses drifting very slowly that catch the sun colour from below, stars (around 90) that fade in as elevation drops below -6 degrees, and a crescent-free night sky with a faint horizon band; three ridges with mist between them, about 30 small pine silhouettes on the nearest ridge, a still pond in the foreground that mirrors the sky colour and the sun as a shimmering vertical column of 14 short horizontal glints, and 12% film grain with a subtle vignette. The sun is a warm disc (#fff3c4 core to #ffd36b to transparent halo) with eight short rays that rotate very slowly. The arc itself is a dashed 2px line (white at 40%) with sunrise and sunset markers on the horizon, labelled. Media slots for real photography: a 1920x1000 horizon photograph that can replace the painted ridges (keep the sky overlay gradient on top at 60% blend), and a 4-second timelapse clip for the sky.

Layout: Card header: eyebrow "Halcyon Daylight", title with the town name and date. Scene: sun arc with horizon line, labels "Sunrise 06:12" and "Sunset 19:48" at the horizon ends, and a "Solar noon" tick at the apex. Control row beneath the scene: a Town select (Pennick Bay 51.5 N, Lantern City 35.7 N, Harbord Reach 63.8 N, Equa Landing 0.5 N, Port Austrelle 33.9 S), a native date input with previous and next day buttons, and a Time slider (00:00 to 23:59, 1-minute steps) with buttons "Sunrise", "Noon", "Sunset" and "Now". Readouts in a four-card grid: Sunrise, Sunset, Daylight length (e.g. 13 h 36 min, with a small bar showing share of the 24 hours), Golden hour (evening window when the sun is between 6 degrees and the horizon, and morning window). A secondary line: "Today is 2 min 14 s longer than yesterday" (or shorter), and the sun's current elevation and a plain label (Night, Twilight, Golden hour, Daytime).

Copy: Polar cases are handled in words: "Midnight sun: the sun does not set today." or "Polar night: the sun does not rise today." with the arc staying wholly above or below the horizon. Footnote: "Approximate solar times ignoring altitude and atmospheric refraction."

Interaction: Elevation uses declination = 23.44 degrees x sin(2 pi (284 + dayOfYear) / 365) and hour angle h = 15 degrees x (time - 12 h): sin(elevation) = sin(lat) sin(dec) + cos(lat) cos(dec) cos(h). Sunrise and sunset are the times where elevation crosses -0.83 degrees (found by scanning in 1-minute steps). Dragging the sun along the arc (pointer events with setPointerCapture) or moving the slider changes the time; sky colours, stars, glow, reflection and the readouts update together. Town and date changes recompute everything and keep the chosen clock time. The draggable sun is role="slider" with arrow keys (1 min), Page keys (1 h), Home and End; changes are announced politely at most once per second. Now sets the real local time, and while the page is in "live" mode the sun advances every 30 seconds. Last town and date are not persisted.

Responsive: Under 760px the scene height becomes 300px, the controls stack, the readout grid is two columns then one at 420px; no horizontal scroll at 390px.

Rules: Single HTML file, Google Fonts link, inline CSS and JS, no libraries or external images. [hidden]{display:none!important}. Visible focus 3px #ffffff outline with dark offset. Sun state is always stated in words as well as colour. Respect prefers-reduced-motion (no drifting clouds or ray rotation, no star twinkle). Invented names only.
